AI in education, described honestly

What holds up across a full teaching term, what quietly stops being opened in week three, and why the difference is rarely about the model.

All writing
  1. Blog
  2. /AI in Education

Most writing about AI in education argues about the wrong thing. The question is not whether a model can explain photosynthesis — it plainly can — but which parts of a teacher’s or a lecturer’s week are worth machine effort, and which parts collapse the moment a person stops being responsible for them. That line moves depending on the task, and locating it is a practical exercise rather than a philosophical one.

The pieces in this category work through that line in specific situations: drafting assessment material, explaining a concept for the fifth time after hours, turning a marksheet into a teaching instruction, and the things that are still sold with more confidence than they deserve. The consistent finding is unexciting. Tools that remove an existing task survive. Tools that ask an educator to change how they teach in exchange for a benefit next term do not.

If you want the product view rather than the argument, the AI layer describes where the models sit and what stays under human review, and the AI tutor covers the after-hours case in detail. Schools and higher education apply the same platform to two different weeks.

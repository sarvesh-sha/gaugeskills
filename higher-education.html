The problem

A transcript records the result. It does not record the skill.

A student clears six semesters at 7.4 CGPA and still stalls in a first-round interview. The marksheet cannot say whether the cause was a core subject from second year, the way the student explains their work, or the four weeks in August when attendance quietly dropped. Placement season is a costly time to find out.

What a department holds by the end of a semester

  • Internal marks and end-semester results
  • An attendance register
  • Faculty impressions, shared in a review meeting
  • A placement spreadsheet assembled in the final year

What it needs by week four

  • Which students are drifting, and on which topics
  • Which concepts a cohort has not absorbed yet
  • Whether each course is on plan against the syllabus
  • Which employability skills are still missing, student by student

Who it is for

Five roles, one record of what a student can do

A department, an academic office and a placement cell currently work from three different spreadsheets. They get one source instead, and each role sees the slice it is accountable for.

  • Students

    A tutor for the concept that did not land in class, and a skill profile that grows with each semester.

  • Faculty

    Course material, question banks and marking support drafted in minutes, approved by the faculty member before use.

  • HODs

    Syllabus coverage, cohort performance and the at-risk list for every course in the department.

  • Academic leadership

    Performance across departments and semesters, and whether last term's intervention actually moved anything.

  • Placement teams

    Placement readiness per student, built from skill assessments rather than from what a CV claims.

Skills and employability

Employability, measured each semester rather than asserted in the final year

A college is judged on placements, and a placement decision is made on what a student can demonstrate. GaugeSkills assesses the skills underneath the degree — aptitude, core subject depth, communication, applied problem solving — and keeps that profile current instead of reconstructing it in the last two months.

  • Skill assessments mapped to the competencies recruiters screen on
  • A profile per student that updates every semester, not once before placements
  • Each gap returned as a practice path, so a student has something to do about it
  • Cohort readiness in the same academic analytics the HOD and placement team already read
  • Re-assessment after the path, so improvement is shown rather than claimed

Questions

What academic teams ask first

Will this replace the academic ERP we already run?

No. The ERP stays the system of record for admissions, enrolment, attendance and results. GaugeSkills reads from it so students, programmes and semesters are not maintained in two places, and adds the layer an ERP was never built for: skill measurement, personalized learning and academic analytics.

How early can it flag an at-risk student?

As soon as there is enough signal to read. Attendance plus the first internal assessment is usually enough to separate a student who is drifting from a student having one difficult week. What the platform produces is a list with a reason attached; the decision to call the student in stays with the faculty member or the HOD.

Does the AI mark student work?

It drafts. Objective questions are scored automatically. For written answers the AI proposes a score and a comment against the rubric the department set, and the faculty member edits and signs it off. We do not put an AI in charge of a university examination result.

How can you measure employability without a recruiter in the room?

By assessing the skills recruiters screen on — aptitude, core subject depth, communication and applied problem solving — against a defined level rather than a self-rating. That gives a placement team evidence to work from in a conversation. It is not a promise of an offer, and we will not describe it as one.

Do faculty have to change how they teach?

No. The syllabus, the course plan and the assessment pattern stay as the department set them. What changes is how long preparation takes, and how much of the result is visible during the semester instead of after it.

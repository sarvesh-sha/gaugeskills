AI for faculty

AI That Gives Faculty Their Week Back

What this actually means

A co-pilot for preparation, not a substitute for a lecturer

Nothing here decides what a course should contain or what a student is worth. The AI produces a first draft of the repetitive artefacts around teaching — a question bank, a revision sheet, a set of worked examples, a summary of who is struggling — and the faculty member edits, rejects or approves it before anyone sees it.

The AI does

  • Drafts material from the syllabus and course content already in use
  • Tags every question with the concept and competency it tests
  • Summarises assessment results into the groups that need the same help
  • Prepares the paperwork around a course rather than the course itself

The faculty member decides

  • What the course covers, in what order, at what depth
  • Whether a generated question is fair, accurate and worth asking
  • What a piece of work is worth, and what feedback a student receives
  • Who needs a conversation, and what that conversation is about

Authorship matters in academia, so the platform never publishes an AI draft on a faculty member's behalf. Read how the AI layer works.

A worked example

One unit, one week, two versions of the same job

This is a single unit of a single course. Nothing here is automated end to end — each row still finishes with the faculty member approving the work.

How the week usually runs

  1. Monday. Rewrite last year's slides because the unit order changed.
  2. Tuesday. Write a class test by hand, mostly by adapting old papers.
  3. Thursday. Mark, record marks, and form a rough impression of who is behind.
  4. Friday. Intend to run a remedial session. Run out of week.

How it runs with a co-pilot

  1. Monday. Slides and a handout come back as drafts against the new unit order. Edit and approve.
  2. Tuesday. Review a generated test, drop two questions, change one, publish.
  3. Thursday. Results arrive already grouped by the concept that failed.
  4. Friday. Run one remedial session for the group that shares the gap.

The point is not speed for its own sake. It is that the remedial session on Friday is the thing that changes a result, and it is the thing that gets cut first when the week is full. See the full faculty workspace.

Questions

What faculty ask before they try it

Who is the author of AI-generated course material?

The faculty member. The platform produces a draft in a workspace only you can see, and nothing reaches a student until you approve it. Most faculty edit heavily on the first few units and less over time as the drafts learn the shape of the course, but the approval step never goes away.

Can the AI mark examinations?

It can assist with objective questions and it can draft feedback on written work, but it does not award final marks on high-stakes assessments on its own. That is a deliberate boundary. Marking carries academic and regulatory weight, and it stays with the examiner.

Will this be used to evaluate faculty performance?

Coverage and pacing data exists because departments ask whether a syllabus was delivered, and heads of department can see it. We are direct about that rather than pretending otherwise. What the platform does not do is score teaching quality or rank faculty against each other from usage data.

Do I have to move my course into a new system?

No. GaugeSkills runs as a complete platform, and it also connects over LTI and SSO, so a department committed to an existing LMS can add the content studio, the question banks and the analytics alongside what it already runs.

The fastest way to judge this is to watch it draft material for a course you already teach. Pick the unit you like preparing least and we will start there.

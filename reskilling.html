What this actually means

The distance between two role standards, for one person

Reskilling is not a longer training course. It is a comparison between where someone is measured today and what a different role requires, which produces three lists: what transfers at level, what transfers but needs raising, and what is genuinely new.

Transfers at level

Skills the person already demonstrates at or above what the target role requires. These are credited rather than retaught, and they are usually the majority.

Transfers, needs raising

Skills held at a lower level than the new role needs. Shorter to close than starting from nothing, because the foundation is already there.

Genuinely new

Skills the person has no measured evidence for. This list is the real cost of the transition, and its length is what makes a move plausible or not.

The size of the third list is the honest test. Where it is short, the transition is a plan. Where it is most of the target role, the person is starting a new career and should be told that plainly rather than enrolled in something optimistic.

How a transition is built

From a role that is shrinking to one that is hiring

  • Step 01

    Find the adjacent roles

    For a given person, compare their measured profile against every role standard in the organization and rank the targets by how little they would have to learn.

  • Step 02

    Credit what already exists

    Skills demonstrated at or above the target level are carried over with their evidence intact. Nobody sits through a module on something they were measured on last quarter.

  • Step 03

    Scope the new skills honestly

    The remaining list is costed in learning time, and it is shown to the person before they commit. A transition nobody has the time for is better declined early.

  • Step 04

    Learn against the target standard

    The path is built from the target role's requirements rather than a generic curriculum, so every module maps to a skill the new role actually needs.

  • Step 05

    Practise in the real context

    Applied tasks and scenarios drawn from the target role, often alongside someone already doing it. Explanation on demand comes from the AI tutor; the mentoring stays human.

  • Step 06

    Re-measure against the new role

    Readiness for the target role is re-assessed at the end, so the decision to move someone rests on a measurement rather than on how the programme felt.

The distinction

Upskilling and reskilling are not the same project

They get budgeted together and they behave completely differently. Confusing them is how an organization ends up running a reskilling programme on an upskilling timeline.

Upskilling

Destination
The same role, performed at a higher level.
Scope
A few skills, usually one or two levels apart.
Trigger
A gap against the standard for the job the person already holds.
Risk if it fails
The work keeps getting escalated to someone more senior.
Read about upskilling

Reskilling

Destination
A different role, with a different standard.
Scope
A set of skills the person has no evidence for at all.
Trigger
A role that is shrinking, a role that cannot be hired for, or a person who wants to move.
Risk if it fails
A vacancy stays open and a capable person leaves the organization.

Questions

What leaders ask before funding a transition

How do you decide which roles someone could move into?

By comparing their measured profile against every role standard you have defined and ranking the targets by how small the remaining list of new skills is. Adjacency falls out of the measurement rather than being asserted by a career map somebody drew three years ago.

Is this something you do to people or with them?

With them, or it does not work. The person sees the same three lists their manager sees, including the honest cost of the new skills, before anything is agreed. A transition someone has not chosen tends to produce an exit rather than a new role.

How long does a reskilling transition take?

It depends entirely on the length of the genuinely-new list, which is exactly why that list is produced first. A move between adjacent roles that share most of a skill set is a different order of commitment from a move across job families, and the analysis tells you which one you are looking at.

What if someone is not ready at the end of the path?

Then the re-assessment says so, which is the point of running it. Options are a longer path, a supported placement in the new role with a reduced initial scope, or a different target. None of those are failures; a programme that declares everyone ready by default is the failure.

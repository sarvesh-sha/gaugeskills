Learning paths

Paths Built From What Someone Is Actually Missing

A course is the same for everyone who enrols in it. A path is the shortest honest route between what a person can demonstrate today and what their class, course or role requires — which means it cannot be written until somebody has been measured.

What this actually means

Personalized is a claim. Scoped by evidence is a mechanism.

Almost every learning product now says personalized, and most of them mean the learner picked a topic from a menu. A path is personalized in a narrower and more useful sense: its contents were determined by what a specific person got wrong.

Scoped by evidence

Modules covering concepts the learner already demonstrated are dropped before the path is issued. What remains is the gap, which is usually a fraction of the course it came from.

Ordered by prerequisite

Concepts have dependencies. A learner failing the current unit is often missing something two units back, so the path starts where the reasoning first broke rather than where the syllabus says.

Changed by what happens

A path is not fixed at assignment. Practice results extend it, shorten it, or insert remediation, so it reflects the learner in week four rather than the learner in week one.

How a path is assembled

  1. 1. Establish the target

    The syllabus for a class, the outcomes for a course, or the standard for a role. Without a defined destination there is nothing to compute a route to.

  2. 2. Measure the starting point

    Assessment establishes where the learner currently stands, concept by concept, rather than asking them what they think they need.

  3. 3. Subtract, then sequence

    The difference becomes the scope; the prerequisite graph turns that scope into an order. Foundations that are missing move to the front even if they belong to an earlier unit.

  4. 4. Fill it with the best content available

    Your own material first, then your licensed library, then generated content where nothing covers the concept. Provenance is visible on every item.

  5. 5. Adapt on evidence, not on clicks

    Practice results drive the changes. Two failed attempts on the same concept insert remediation; a clean pass collapses the rest of that branch.

  6. 6. Close with a measurement

    The path ends in a re-assessment of the concepts it targeted. Finishing the content is not the outcome; moving the level is.

What changes

Shorter routes finish more often

  • Nobody sits through a module on something they can already do.

    Skipping demonstrated concepts typically removes a large part of a course. The learning that remains is the learning that was needed, and it fits in the time people actually have.

    Gap-scoped content

  • Fix the foundation instead of repeating the current chapter.

    When the prerequisite graph finds a missing concept two units back, the path goes there first, which is why the third explanation of the current topic finally lands.

    Prerequisite sequencing

  • Help arrives at the exact point someone gets stuck.

    The AI tutor sits inside the path and explains the step that broke, using the institution's own material. It supports the learner between sessions rather than replacing the teacher or coach.

    In-path AI tutor

  • Teachers and managers see where a path stalled, and why.

    A path that has not moved in two weeks is visible as a stalled commitment against a named person, which is a different thing from a low completion percentage in a report.

    Progress visibility

  • Get more out of the content you already licensed.

    Paths are assembled from your existing library and internal material. The value added is the targeting and the measurement around the content, not another catalogue.

    Bring your own content

  • End with proof rather than a completion tick.

    The closing re-assessment produces a second measurement on the same concepts, so the report says which levels moved instead of how many people finished.

    Closing re-assessment

Three audiences

The same mechanism, three vocabularies

A path is computed the same way everywhere: target, measurement, subtraction, sequence, adapt, re-measure. What changes is what the target is made of and what the learner sees, because a Class 8 student and a mid-career engineer are not doing the same thing.

Schools

Against the class syllabus

The target is the concepts in the subject for that class. Paths are short, run alongside the timetable, and are mostly remediation: the topic from last term that never got solid and is now blocking this one.

Higher Education

Against course outcomes

The target is the outcomes a course declares, plus the competencies a placement team cares about. Paths run across a semester and often serve a group of students who failed the same concept together.

Enterprise

Against a role standard

Questions

What people ask about paths

Is a learning path just a playlist of courses?

A playlist is a fixed list somebody curated in advance. A path is computed for one learner from a measurement, ordered by prerequisites rather than by catalogue structure, and changed by what happens during it. The practical difference shows up in length: a curated playlist covers a subject, a path covers a gap, and the gap is usually much smaller.

Do we have to use your content?

No. Paths are assembled from whatever you have — your own material, an existing licensed library, or content generated where nothing covers a concept. Every item shows where it came from. What the platform contributes is the targeting, the sequencing and the measurement, which is the part a content library does not do.

What happens when a learner gets stuck halfway through?

Two failed attempts on the same concept insert remediation ahead of the next module rather than letting the learner continue and fail again. The AI tutor is available at that point to explain the step differently. If the pattern repeats, the path is flagged to the teacher, faculty member or manager responsible, because at that point it is a teaching problem rather than a content problem.

Can a teacher or manager change the path?

Yes, and they should be able to. A computed path is a strong starting point built from evidence, but it does not know that a student has been unwell or that a role is changing next quarter. Modules can be added, removed or reordered, and the change is attributed so the reasoning survives the person who made it.

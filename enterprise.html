The problem

Your org chart says what people were hired to do. It does not say what they can do.

A function is asked to take on a new platform next quarter. Nobody can say how many people are already close, how many need four weeks, and how many are starting from zero. The planning cycle below is the one most organizations run, and it fails at the same point every time.

  1. Step 1

    A capability is committed to

    Leadership takes on a new platform, a new market or an AI programme, with a date attached to it.

  2. Step 2

    Skills are estimated

    HR circulates a self-assessment. Managers fill in what they remember. Nobody treats the result as reliable.

  3. Step 3

    Training is bought

    A catalogue is licensed and the whole function is enrolled, whatever each person's starting point was.

  4. Step 4

    Completion is reported

    The review deck shows hours and completion rates. It cannot show that capability moved, because nothing was measured first.

Step two is where the rest of the cycle goes wrong. GaugeSkills replaces the estimate with a measurement, and everything downstream — the spend, the paths, the report to leadership — starts pointing at something real.

Who it is for

One skills record, five different questions asked of it

An employee, a manager and a leadership team are not looking for the same thing. Each gets the view that matches the decision they have to make, and nothing beyond it.

  • Employees

    A measured skill profile, the gaps between it and the role they want next, and a path aimed at those gaps.

  • Managers

    Where the team is strong, where it is short, and who is close to ready for the work coming next quarter.

  • HR

    A skills inventory that matches the org chart, kept current from the HRMS rather than from a survey.

  • L&D

    Programmes aimed at measured gaps, and re-assessment that shows what each programme actually changed.

  • Leadership

    Workforce readiness by function and role, and which capabilities the organization should build first.

Skills measurement

A skills inventory you can defend in a planning meeting

Everything else on this page depends on the measurement being credible. So a skill level is produced by assessment against a published definition, tied to the role framework HR already maintains, and re-taken on a schedule — because an inventory that is accurate once is a document, not a system.

  • Employee skill assessment against a defined level, not a five-point self-rating
  • Competency assessment mapped to the role definitions your HR team already owns
  • Certifications recorded with their expiry, so a compliance-critical skill does not lapse unnoticed
  • Manager dashboards scoped to their own team; workforce analytics for HR and leadership
  • Scheduled re-assessment, so the inventory stays current after the first rollout

Questions

What HR and L&D ask first

Is this another LMS?

No. An LMS delivers training and records that it happened. GaugeSkills starts one step earlier, by measuring what people can actually do against what their roles require, and finishes one step later, by re-assessing to show whether the training changed anything. It can run as the whole platform or sit alongside an LMS you already own.

How do you assess skills without taking a week off everyone's calendar?

Assessments are scoped to the competencies a role is defined by, so an employee answers for the skills that matter to their role rather than working through a catalogue. Most sittings are short, and a measured skill level carries forward, so moving between roles re-uses evidence instead of re-collecting it.

Will employees trust a system that puts a level on them?

That depends on how it is introduced. A skill level in GaugeSkills is visible to the employee, it arrives with the learning path that closes the gap, and it is a development input rather than a performance rating. We would not recommend using it as one, and the product is not built to support that.

Does it connect to our HRMS and identity provider?

Yes. People, roles, departments and reporting lines sync from the HRMS so the skills view matches the org chart, and sign-in runs through your existing identity provider with central provisioning. Which specific systems apply depends on what you run, which is a technical session rather than a claim on a web page.

Who reviews what the AI produces?

A person, every time. The assistant drafts learning content, summarises assessment results and proposes a path. L&D owns what gets published, the manager owns the development conversation, and no AI output reaches an employee without someone approving it first.

Start with one function, not the whole company

Pick a team and a role you are trying to staff. We will assess against that role definition, show the gaps and the paths that follow, and you can judge the platform on its output rather than on a pitch.
